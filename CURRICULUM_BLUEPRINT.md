# MY ENGLISH — Метод Холмата
## Curriculum Blueprint v1.0

**Purpose:** build practical English ability for independent life and work in Europe. The course is open-ended: the domains and situations below are the initial map, not a maximum list. New situations can be added without changing the learning engine.

## Product architecture

### Main program: Жизненные ситуации
The primary learning path. Organize content as:
1. Life domain
2. Situation family
3. Specific scenario
4. Lessons and practice stages
5. Scenario assessment
6. Domain assessment
7. Cumulative review

### Supporting tools (keep existing app sections)
- Grammar reference and focused grammar practice
- Global vocabulary catalogue
- Flashcards and custom collections
- Vocabulary checks
- Review of weak areas
- Progress and assessment history

Grammar is taught in context in the main path and remains available as a separate systematic course.

## Initial life-domain map (expandable)

1. Personal identity and everyday conversation: introductions, personal details, family, interests, small talk, opinions, plans.
2. Job search and employment: finding vacancies, applications, CV, interview, experience, skills, availability, salary, contract, documents, first day, duties, shifts, safety, colleagues, managers, feedback, conflict, resignation.
3. Housing: searching, viewing, rent, deposit, contract, utilities, repairs, landlord, neighbours, moving, household rules.
4. Shopping and services: groceries, clothes, sizes, prices, payment, returns, complaints, delivery, appointments, hairdresser, repairs.
5. Transport and navigation: walking directions, public transport, tickets, schedules, delays, transfers, taxi, driving, parking, breakdowns.
6. Documents and public services: registration, residence/work paperwork, appointments, forms, official questions, explaining a problem, requesting clarification.
7. Healthcare: booking, symptoms, medical history, pharmacy, instructions, emergencies, insurance and follow-up.
8. Banking and money: opening an account, cards, cash, transfers, bills, salary payments, fees, budgeting, tax-related conversations.
9. Food and hospitality: cafés, restaurants, ordering, dietary requests, reservations, hotels, complaints.
10. Communication and technology: phone calls, messages, email, internet, mobile plans, troubleshooting, online accounts.
11. Social and community life: neighbours, invitations, visits, friendships, celebrations, polite disagreement, cultural misunderstandings.
12. Education and learning: courses, teachers, enrolment, instructions, asking questions, examinations.
13. Family and personal responsibilities: school communication, childcare arrangements, family logistics and everyday requests.
14. Travel and accommodation: planning, booking, airport, border questions, lost luggage, cancellations and changes.
15. Safety and unexpected events: lost property, theft, accidents, urgent help, misunderstandings, making a complaint.
16. Civic and cultural life: local rules, community events, news, public information and respectful discussion.
17. Work-specific English: tools, equipment, processes, instructions, quality, deadlines, health and safety. Add industry-specific branches when the learner's target job is known.
18. Advanced real-life communication: negotiations, explaining complex problems, persuasion, presentations, abstract topics, nuanced opinions and difficult conversations.

This map is deliberately expandable. Do not reject a useful scenario because it is not listed here.

## Standard structure for every scenario lesson

1. **Outcome:** what the learner must be able to do in a real interaction.
2. **Context:** who is speaking, where, why, and what may happen.
3. **Vocabulary table:** English, IPA transcription, Russian meaning, audio control, example and translation. Explain words before testing them.
4. **Pronunciation lab:** listen, repeat, record, compare recognized words/sounds where supported, retry. Do not claim speech recognition is a perfect accent evaluator.
5. **Flashcards:** English-to-Russian and Russian-to-English, spaced review.
6. **Useful language:** natural phrases, polite forms, clarification and repair phrases.
7. **Contextual grammar:** rule, form, meaning, common errors, contrast with previously learned forms.
8. **Guided sentence building:** word order, prompts, transformations, then independent production.
9. **Listening comprehension:** short utterances, longer dialogues, varied speakers/speeds where audio assets permit; questions about meaning, intention and details.
10. **Dialogue analysis:** line-by-line comprehension, useful alternatives, follow-up questions.
11. **Varied exercises:** selected dynamically from a large bank; do not repeat one template with superficial word substitutions.
12. **Role-play:** learner must respond without seeing a prepared answer; include follow-up and unexpected turns.
13. **Scenario challenge:** complete the real-world task from beginning to end.
14. **Review:** errors, vocabulary due for review, grammar to revisit, next recommended step.

## Grammar progression

Grammar is cumulative and spiral: previously learned forms return in later lessons and are combined with new forms.

### A1 — Foundations
- Sentence structure; subject and object pronouns; possessives and demonstratives
- Verb be; articles; singular/plural; basic noun phrases
- Have got / have; there is/are; basic prepositions
- Present Simple: affirmative, negative, questions, third-person forms
- Present Continuous; contrast with Present Simple
- Can/can't; imperatives; basic requests
- Wh- questions; question word order
- Basic conjunctions, frequency expressions, time and place phrases

### A2 — Everyday independence
- Past Simple: regular/irregular verbs, questions and negatives
- Past Continuous and contrast with Past Simple
- Future plans: going to, Present Continuous for arrangements, will
- Present Perfect foundations and contrast with Past Simple
- Countable/uncountable nouns; quantifiers; articles in broader contexts
- Comparatives/superlatives; adverbs; manner and frequency
- Modals: must, have to, should, could, may, might
- Infinitive and gerund foundations; object pronouns; possessive forms
- Basic relative clauses, conjunctions and time clauses

### B1 — Independent communication
- Present Perfect vs Past Simple; for/since, already/yet/just
- Present Perfect Continuous foundations
- Narrative tense combinations; Past Perfect foundations
- Future forms and time clauses
- First and second conditionals; unless
- Passive voice across common tenses
- Reported speech foundations and reporting verbs
- Relative clauses; defining/non-defining basics
- Gerunds/infinitives and verb patterns
- Modal deductions and degrees of obligation
- Linking devices, clauses, indirect questions and question tags

### B2 — Flexible and precise communication
- Full tense/aspect contrasts in context
- Past Perfect Continuous and advanced narrative sequencing
- Third and mixed conditionals
- Advanced passive structures and causative have/get
- Reported speech with tense/modal changes
- Modal perfect forms (might have, should have, must have)
- Advanced gerund/infinitive patterns
- Participle clauses, reduced relative clauses and complex clauses
- Inversion and emphasis where useful
- Hedging, stance, concession, contrast and precise linking
- Register: formal, neutral, informal; natural spoken grammar and common phrasal patterns

The sequence is a curriculum framework, not a claim that every learner masters a level by completing a checklist. Placement and progression depend on demonstrated performance.

## Exercise engine: variety and progression

Use multiple exercise families and rotate them:
- Multiple choice and distractor diagnosis
- Gap fill with one or several blanks
- English-to-Russian and Russian-to-English translation
- Sentence assembly from shuffled chunks
- Independent sentence writing
- Question formation and short/extended answers
- Affirmative/negative/question transformations
- Error detection and correction with explanation
- Choose the correct tense/form from context
- Rewrite while preserving meaning
- Dialogue completion with multiple valid answers
- Listening for gist, detail, intention and implied meaning
- Reading comprehension and information extraction
- Describe a picture/event/process when suitable content exists
- Role-play with adaptive follow-up questions
- Real-world task simulation and integrated tests

Difficulty steps:
1. Recognition with clear support
2. Controlled production
3. Production with fewer hints
4. Mixed practice with older material
5. Time-limited or listening-led response
6. Open-ended communication
7. Transfer to a new, unfamiliar context

Do not label a set as hundreds of unique exercises unless that many genuinely distinct, validated items exist. Track item identity and avoid near-duplicate repetition.

## Mastery and progression rules

Track separately:
- Vocabulary recall in both directions
- Grammar accuracy by skill and construction
- Listening comprehension
- Sentence production
- Speaking task completion and intelligibility indicators
- Role-play independence
- Retention after a delay

A learner should not advance solely by tapping through a lesson. Use a configurable mastery threshold, mixed review, and a short applied task. If a skill is weak, assign targeted practice and retest it in a new context. Keep progress persistent and do not silently reset it.

## Data model direction

Keep lesson content separate from Streamlit UI. Store structured content with stable IDs:
- domain_id
- situation_id
- lesson_id
- vocabulary item IDs
- grammar point IDs
- exercise IDs and exercise type
- dialogue/audio asset references
- rubric and mastery requirements

Each exercise must declare its answer type, accepted answers, explanation, difficulty, skills tested, and prerequisite knowledge. Never test unexplained vocabulary or grammar.

## Technical principles

- Extend the existing Streamlit application; do not create a parallel app.
- Preserve existing vocabulary, flashcards, grammar, custom collections, and progress features.
- Do not reintroduce Gemini.
- Avoid breaking current SQLite data; any database migration must be explicit and backward-compatible.
- Test speech input/output in the actual target browser and Streamlit deployment before promising availability.
- Keep speech recognition feedback honest: recognition confidence and transcript match are not a complete phonetic/accent score.
- Build and validate one complete reference module before scaling content.
- Add content in small verified stages; inspect the deployed app after each change.
